# Repriced Before the Robots Arrived: AI, BPO, and the New Economics of Customer Experience

*Investors are writing off the call-center giants on a bet that AI will gut CX costs. The math is real. But the winners will not be whoever runs the cheapest bot.*

*Draft for the TransPerfect Financial Services blog · What's making headlines · 2026-09-22*

---

The most telling story in customer experience this year is not a product launch. It is a repricing. Markets are [marking down the call-center giants before AI has finished the job](https://startupfortune.com/call-center-giants-are-being-repriced-out-of-existence-before-ai-has-finished-the-job/), dumping shares in outsourcing leaders like Teleperformance and Concentrix on the bet that automation will hollow out their core business. The logic is brutal and simple: the human cost floor for a customer interaction has sat at roughly $6 to $12 for years, while AI is pushing the marginal cost of a resolved ticket below $1.50. In a [BPO market worth more than $280 billion](https://www.parloa.com/knowledge-hub/top-bpo-companies/), that spread is enough to reprice an entire industry.

The forecasts behind the sell-off are not fringe. Gartner has projected that conversational AI would [reduce contact-center agent labor costs by $80 billion by 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2022-08-31-gartner-predicts-conversational-ai-will-reduce-contac), noting that labor can account for up to 95% of contact-center costs. More recently, it predicted that [agentic AI will autonomously resolve 80% of common customer service issues without human intervention by 2029](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-03-05-gartner-predicts-agentic-ai-will-autonomously-resolve-80-percent-of-common-customer-service-issues-without-human-intervention-by-20290), cutting operational costs by around 30%. This is no longer scripted chatbots. Today's [agentic systems take multi-step actions on their own](https://execsintheknow.com/the-top-5-ways-agentic-ai-is-transforming-total-experience-in-the-contact-center/), authenticating a customer, updating a billing record, issuing a refund, and sending confirmation without an agent touching it.

## The catch the sell-off is ignoring

Here is where the headline gets more interesting than the stock chart. The savings are real, but the wholesale replacement of humans is running well ahead of what is actually happening. In Gartner's own surveying, [executive pressure to deploy AI is near-universal while actual staffing cuts are not](https://www.veribl.com/blog/gartner-agentic-ai-customer-service-2026): roughly nine in ten service leaders report pressure from the top to adopt AI, yet only about one in five have genuinely reduced headcount because of it. Gartner goes further, expecting that half of the organizations that cut staff citing AI will rehire for similar work, under new titles, within a couple of years.

Why the gap between the forecast and the floor? Because the cheap ticket and the good outcome are not the same product. Automating a password reset is easy. Handling a disputed charge, a fraud alert, a hardship case, or a confused first-time borrower is not, and getting it wrong in financial services is expensive in ways a per-interaction cost model does not capture. The industry's own emerging answer is not "bots instead of people" but a [tandem model in which AI and human agents operate as one coordinated system](https://execsintheknow.com/the-top-5-ways-agentic-ai-is-transforming-total-experience-in-the-contact-center/), with the handoffs invisible to the customer.

## Where the moat actually is

If AI collapses the cost of a basic answer to near zero, the value migrates to the things it cannot yet do cheaply and consistently: judgment, trust, and language. That last one is underrated. In 2026, [the question is no longer whether a support AI can speak a customer's language](https://www.lorikeetcx.ai/articles/best-multilingual-ai-customer-support-2026), nearly all of them can generate text in dozens. The real test is whether it resolves with the same accuracy, tone, compliance guardrails, and audit trail across every language, and whether it can switch languages mid-conversation when the customer does.

That parity gap is the moat, and it is widest in regulated, multilingual customer bases. A bank that deflects 80% of English tickets but mishandles the same interaction in Spanish, Vietnamese, or Haitian Creole has not cut costs; it has moved its churn and its compliance risk into the languages it measures least. The winners of the repricing will be the operations that pair agentic automation with human oversight and consistent, governed multilingual quality, so a customer gets the same trustworthy answer whether a bot or a person, and whatever language, delivers it.

The market is right that CX economics are being rewritten. It is wrong if it thinks the prize goes to the cheapest bot. It goes to whoever keeps quality, trust, and language intact while the cost of everything else falls.
