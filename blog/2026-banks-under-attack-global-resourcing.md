# Under Attack and Understaffed: Resourcing the Bank's Highest-Stakes Work

*The adversary is now a nation-state that steals billions with near-impunity, and for the institutions that safeguard assets and data, the response is a talent problem. The ones that stay ahead treat flexible, global expertise as core infrastructure.*

*Draft for the TransPerfect Financial Services blog · 2026-11-02*

---

The institutions that safeguard the world's assets and data have never been more exposed. On October 29, 2026, attackers drained [about $6.3 billion from Binance in roughly twenty minutes](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/30/binance-says-6-3bn-stolen-in-biggest-crypto-heist-in-history-what-we-know), the largest crypto theft on record, and within days the [FBI attributed it to TraderTraitor](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/business-news/fbi-says-north-korea-was-6-billion-binance-crypto-hack-rcna606758), a state-sponsored North Korean unit tied to the Lazarus Group. The way in was not an exotic cryptographic break. It was [an engineer's laptop](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/30/billions-in-crypto-frozen-in-binance-hack-heres-what-happens-next-.html).

## When the attacker is a nation-state

For any institution moving into digital-asset custody, that is not a crypto story. It is a preview. The adversary is no longer only criminals; it is nation-states fielding elite units that treat theft as statecraft, and they operate with near-impunity. Washington sanctioned the group back in 2019 and the thefts have only grown, now [past $12.8 billion since 2017](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/north-korea-steals-usd-6-36-billion-from-binance-in-the-largest-hack-in-history), with no arrests and little recovered. When you cannot deter the attacker, the entire burden shifts onto the target, and out-executing a state demands a caliber of expertise that is extraordinarily scarce.

The stakes are not back-office. Under SEC rules a public company must [disclose a material incident within four business days](https://incidentcost.com/industries/financial-services), turning a breach into an immediate share-price event, while brand, client trust, and regulatory standing all move at once. For a custodian, whose entire promise is safekeeping, a breach strikes the core of the franchise, and it is judged on how fast and how well the response is executed.

That response is, at bottom, a talent problem, and the skills are scarce: in the latest industry study, [88% of organizations reported a significant consequence from a cybersecurity skills shortage](https://www.isc2.org/Insights/2025/12/2025-ISC2-Cybersecurity-Workforce-Study). The need is also uneven. A breach or a regulatory lookback demands a large expert team now, for weeks; a transformation needs a sustained bench for years. No institution can carry all of that as permanent headcount, nor recruit it the moment a crisis hits.

## Expertise as infrastructure

The answer is to treat access to expertise as infrastructure. A partner with genuine global reach can keep the whole incident-response cycle under one roof, where competitors cover only a slice: from digital-forensic collection and data mining, through detecting and extracting exposed personal information and consolidating it into a clean, de-duplicated notification population, to the notices, call-center monitoring, and credit-monitoring that follow. That work can run onshore or offshore and stay in-region, in EU, UK, or US data centers, to meet residency and government requirements, with AI used only where a carrier is comfortable having it near sensitive data.

## Turning a permanent threat into a managed one

Custodians will not stop being targets, and the obligations of scale will not get lighter. Resilience, then, is not only a matter of better defenses. It is the capacity to mobilize the right expertise at the right moment, again and again, on the work where brand, share price, and regulatory standing are all on the line at once. The institutions that build that capacity, or partner for it, turn a permanent state of being under attack into something they can actually manage.
